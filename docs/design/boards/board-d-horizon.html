01 · What this merges

Field Day's planet, Plain Sight's screens, After Dark's discipline.

Sai's read on the three boards: B and C are the cleaner systems, A has the idea worth branding around. The sky, the hill, and something resting on it feel organic because the app connects people with their interests and with each other. The one problem is the ball. This board keeps the planet and swaps the ball for five candidates that cannot be read as a sport.

From A · Field Day

The mark. A round planet: sky above, a hill below, one orange thing. The same scene is the default event cover, so a chapter with no photos still looks like it belongs.

From C · Plain Sight

The system. White surfaces, Figtree, round avatars, soft tints for states, 16 px cards, pill buttons, true black in dark mode. Warmth comes from people and photos.

From B · After Dark

The rule. One loud color, and only the Going button and live states get it. Its text is ink, never white. Dark mode is a first-class design, not an inversion.

02 · The mark

Five ways to take the ball off the hill.

Every candidate keeps the exact planet from board A: the same sky, the same hill, the same orange. Only the object changes. Each is shown at 112 px, as an app icon, at 40, 24 and 16 px, and in one color.

Recommended

1Sunrise

The orange disc drops behind the crest, so it is a sun coming up over the hill: a day about to happen. A ball cannot be half inside a hill, which is the whole trick. Biggest shape of the five, so it survives 16 px and a notification badge.

Risk · Reads as "morning" more than "evening"; a pull-to-refresh where the sun rises a little is the obvious motion.

40 · 24 · 16 · mono
2Ring

The sun stays in the sky but is hollow. A ring is a sun, a planet, a halo, a circle of people; it is never a ball. The ring also becomes the "new this week" indicator around hub avatars, so the mark and the UI share a shape.

Risk · The hollow collapses below 20 px; the 16 px version needs a thicker stroke or a solid dot.

40 · 24 · 16 · mono
3Two on the hill

No object at all. Two people sit on the crest, which is the literal product: you and someone else, in a place, because of a thing you both like. The most honest of the five and the one that says "social" without saying "sport".

Risk · At 16 px two figures become one orange bump; the favicon and tab icon would use the app-icon crop instead.

40 · 24 · 16 · mono
4Pin

A location pin planted on the crest: here, at this time. Pairs naturally with "Classroom South 200" and "25 Park Place" copy and with the map in the event sheet.

Risk · Pins belong to maps apps; it is the most generic of the five and the hardest to own.

40 · 24 · 16 · mono
5Trail

A path runs over the hill toward a small sun: somewhere to go. The most "outdoors" of the five, and the only one with a line, which gives the brand a stroke to animate along.

Risk · Three elements is one too many at small sizes; the path disappears at 16 px and the small sun drifts back toward "ball".

Why 1 over the rest. Sunrise keeps the biggest, simplest silhouette (two shapes and a horizon), it is the only one that still works as a 16 px favicon and inside a notification dot, and it carries a meaning the product wants: something is about to happen today. Ring is the strongest alternative because the hollow shape repeats as the hub-avatar "new" indicator. Two on the hill is the best story and the worst favicon; if you love it, it can be the app icon while 1 is the small mark, since they share the same planet.

07 · Rules the code will enforce

Six rules, then tokens.

One loud thing per screen.

The accent is for the primary action and live states. A screen with two orange buttons is a bug.

Ink on the accent, never white.

White on the sun orange is 3.3:1 and fails. Ink is 5.8:1 in light and 7.4:1 in dark.

Sky and grass are a picture.

They appear in the mark and in default covers. They are never text, borders, buttons or chips.

States have a word and a hue.

Green only means you're in, raspberry only means it is happening, amber is capacity, red is the end of something, grey-blue is "came from PIN".

No gradients, no glows, no blur.

Depth comes from one soft shadow in light mode and from surface steps in dark mode.

Real rooms, real counts.

Classroom South 200, 25 Park Place, 4 of 6. A plan with a room number is a plan you show up to.

Everything on this page becomes packages/ui/src/tokens.css: the surfaces, the accent and its text and tint, the five state pairs, the three planet colors, the ten hub hues, the radii and the type scale. The only thing waiting on a decision is which object sits on the hill. Pick a number and the mark ships as an SVG component, a favicon, a PWA icon set and the default event cover.
