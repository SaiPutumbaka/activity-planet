02 · Logo

The field planet

A planet that is just sky, a hill, and a ball resting on it. No birds, no mountains, no gradient; the three old-logo colors are kept, the illustration is not.

24 px nav Activity Planet
One color
App icon

The mark is the view from the ground on a good day: sky on top, a hill in the lower third, a ball sitting on the crest where you would actually be standing. On the app icon the same scene fills the whole tile, so the icon reads as a place rather than a badge, and the ball lands in the spot a notification would.

04 · Type

Scoreboard + jersey

Barlow Semi Condensed for anything you read from a distance: event titles, section headers, the date block. Rubik for everything you read up close: body, labels, chat. Both from Google Fonts; both have the squared-off friendliness of a gym sign, so they look like one voice at two volumes.

07 · Motion

Fast, physical, quiet

Nothing floats, nothing glows. Motion exists to confirm that a tap landed and to make the next thing appear where your eye already is. Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion, which replaces every transform with an instant swap and keeps the color change.

Press
scale 1 → 0.97 · 90 ms ease-out on touch-down · back to 1 in 180 ms cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)

Every button and card. No color flash on press; the shrink is the feedback. Haptic light on release for Going only.

RSVP success
fill orange → green 220 ms ease-in-out · check draws in 180 ms · count ticks 4 → 5 in 240 ms · your avatar slides into the stack 260 ms cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)

The button changes color in place, never size. The one overshoot in the system is your avatar arriving, because that is the moment the product is about.

Tab switch
outgoing view fades 0 → 1 over 120 ms linear · icon outline → fill 120 ms · no horizontal slide · scroll position restored per tab

Instant, like Instagram. A slide would imply the tabs are a sequence; they are not.

Pull to refresh
the mark's horizon tilts with pull distance, up to 18° at 72 px · on release the ball rolls down the hill in a 600 ms loop until data lands · settle 200 ms ease-out

The logo is the spinner, which is the only place the brand animates. The loop is linear so it never looks like it is about to finish.

Chat send
bubble appears from the composer: translateY 12 → 0 px and opacity 0 → 1 over 200 ms cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1) · composer clears instantly · send button fills orange 120 ms when text exists, back to surface-2 when empty

Optimistic, no spinner. If the send fails the bubble gets a "Tap to retry" in danger text under it, still in place.

08 · Why this is not AI slop

Choices you can point at

The Going button is ink on orange, not white on a gradient; that is the basketball and the referee's shirt, and it is also why it passes AA while white text would not. The logo is three flat shapes with a story (sky, hill, a ball you could pick up) instead of a swoosh, and the same scene becomes the default event cover so a chapter with no photos still looks like it belongs. The display face is Barlow Semi Condensed, a jersey number, not a tech-startup grotesk; the UI face is Rubik because its soft corners read on a phone in daylight. There are no glows, no blur orbs, no marquee, no hero stats; the only ring on the page means "this hub has a new event this week". Hubs get ten named hues with two-stroke glyphs so a Table Tennis chapter is not a paddle emoji in a grey circle. Every state has a separate hue and a separate word: green only means you're in, raspberry only means it's happening, amber is capacity, red is the end of something, steel blue is "came from PIN". The copy uses real rooms (Classroom South 200, 25 Park Place) and real numbers (4 of 6, happens at 4) because a plan with a room number is a plan you show up to. Dark mode is cool near-black, not navy, not purple, with the orange turned up one stop so it stays the loudest thing on screen. And the one gradient the brief allowed is not used at all.
