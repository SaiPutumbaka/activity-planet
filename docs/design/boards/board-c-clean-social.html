Motion

Fast, physical, and over before you notice

Everything under 300ms, one easing family, and nothing moves that you did not touch. prefers-reduced-motion replaces every transform with an opacity swap.

Press

Any button or card scales to 0.97 in 90ms ease-out on touch-down and returns in 180ms cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1) on release. Opacity never changes; the dim-on-press look is for web buttons, not apps. Light haptic on primary only.

RSVP success

Tap Going: the label crossfades to the check in 120ms, the check stroke draws in 240ms cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1), the fill shifts blue to green tint over the same 240ms, and your avatar slides into the stack from the right in 260ms with a 60ms delay. The toast rises 220ms after that. Total felt time: a third of a second. Medium haptic at the check.

Tab switch

No horizontal slide. Outgoing view fades out in 120ms, incoming fades in over 160ms ease-out starting at 80ms; the icon stroke thickens and recolors in 120ms. Tapping the active tab scrolls to top in 320ms cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1).

Pull to refresh

Rubber-band with 0.5 resistance. As you pull, the brand ring draws itself clockwise and the dot appears when you cross 72px; release past that and the ring spins at 900ms linear until data lands, then settles in 300ms ease-out. New cards insert with a 140ms fade; nothing bounces.

Chat send

The composer clears instantly. The bubble appears at the composer position and moves up 12px into place while fading in, 180ms ease-out; the timestamp fades in 120ms later. Incoming messages do the same from 8px. Failed sends shake ±4px twice in 240ms and show Retry.

Why this is not AI slop

The choices, named

There is exactly one saturated color and it has one job: on the Home screen you can count the blue (a story ring, an active tab, and the buttons you have not answered), and the moment you say yes the button turns a quiet green so the next decision is the loudest thing again. Dark mode is true black with hairlines instead of shadows, the way Instagram actually does it, not a navy approximation. The type is one family, Figtree, chosen because its rounded terminals read friendly at 32px and crisp at 13px, with tabular figures on every time and count; it is not Inter, there is no serif display face, and nothing is in uppercase except a 40px date column where caps earn their keep. There are two radii in the whole system, pill and 16, and every control is at least 44px tall. The five state hues were picked so that live (rose) and cancelled (red) are never confused, and categories are tints behind glyphs rather than emoji or saturated fills, so eight hubs on one screen still read as white. The logo is a flat, one-color open ring with a dot in the gap, the same picture as every "4 of 6 going" stack in the app, drawn to survive 16px; there is no gradient anywhere, including the icon. The copy is from the actual place: PIN, PantherCard, the Rec on Piedmont, Library South, Piedmont North, a snow-day cancellation in Atlanta in January. Nothing glows, nothing blurs, no card floats on a gradient, no section is numbered, and no screen has a hero stat. What remains is people's first names, where to be, and one button.
